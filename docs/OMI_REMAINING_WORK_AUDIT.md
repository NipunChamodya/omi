## Findings, remediation and fresh verification

A usable queue or menu does not by itself certify public deployment, physical-phone behavior or an approved multi-round match lifecycle.

### P0: Rule-gated progression and match ending

Relevant files: `docs/OWNER_RULES.md`, `docs/RULE_CLARIFICATIONS.md`, `packages/game-engine/src/scoring.ts`, `apps/server/src/multiplayer/{rooms,projection}.ts`, shared protocol and `GameTable.tsx`.

Current behavior: one round completes and the server awards approved 1/2/3-point outcomes or a single draw carry. A new production round cannot choose its dealer; some later scoring combinations also remain unresolved. Expected behavior is consecutive server-owned rounds and an authoritative terminal match outcome. The root cause is missing approved policies, not a missing button callback. Required changes after owner decisions are a documented ordinary dealer policy, exact carry combinations and a terminal match/tie policy, with corresponding server result events and win/loss audio triggers. Accusation/no-trump/replacement policies remain separately open.

Independent fixes implemented: snapshots now expose a server-derived continuation capability and the UI disables Next Round with the exact missing dealer-rule explanation. Disconnected players also disable the next-round control. The backend continues enforcing rules even if the UI is bypassed. No unapproved policy was enabled, and no match-winning score was guessed.

Coverage: a new real-WebSocket integration completes **three full consecutive rounds**, all 96 card plays, cumulative scoring and four private projections under a clearly labeled hypothetical server-only dealer policy. It checks host permissions, duplicate action acknowledgements, stale versions, disconnect/reconnect before advancing and unique round IDs. Its raised HTTP fixture allowance avoids conflating rapid synthetic snapshot polling with production rate limits; production limits are unchanged. These are mechanics tests, not approval of the fixture policy or a production complete-match claim.

### P0: Back navigation and accidental abandonment

Relevant files: `game/client.ts`, `App.tsx`, `Home.tsx`, `MultiplayerMenu.tsx`, client and browser tests.

Previously history was replaced rather than traversed, menu routes were absent, and Home immediately stopped game transport. Expected behavior is meaningful Back/browser-history navigation without reloading, creating rooms, losing authoritative state or silently abandoning an active table.

Implemented same-document Home/Multiplayer/Friends/Create/Join/Online/Room history, validated history entries, a cancellable native dialog before leaving an active round/result, and native beforeunload protection for reload/tab departure after user activation. Cancelling browser Back restores the forward room entry without reopening transport; confirming closes local transports while retaining the saved seat. Authentication logout still force-clears local identity, and an assigned account recovers its own room through the existing authenticated session/snapshot paths. Menus never create rooms on mount.

Coverage: unit tests verify navigation without create requests, confirmation preserving the live socket, and confirmed leave retaining the same snapshot/seat. Chromium/Firefox browser tests exercise the menu hierarchy, browser Back, active-game Home/Back cancellation, intentional reload and correct same-hand recovery. Android/iOS hardware history gestures remain unverified; browser-engine tests do not establish platform certification.

### P0: Full-screen mobile play

Relevant files: `App.tsx`, `styles.css`, game browser checks. Root cause: the old page included settings/history/voice beneath the game and used hand-fit checks without proving a bounded document.

Implemented a `100dvh` game stage with safe-area padding and bounded grid rows. Scores/trump, table, four seats, hand and play control remain in the stage. Settings, voice, social controls and history remain accessible through their own scrollable surfaces; the document itself does not scroll during gameplay. Voice/social components retain their identity and mounting position across lobby/game transitions. Narrow hands use two rows of at least 44px cards instead of shrinking/overlapping interactive targets. Landscape places hand and table alongside each other. Marketing footer removal during play does not remove a game control.

Coverage: document-height/width, card containment, minimum card dimensions, pairwise non-overlap and essential-control viewport assertions at **320×568, 375×667, 390×844, 393×852, 412×915, 430×932, 667×375 and 844×390**. These are representative CSS viewports, including a tall Samsung-class viewport; they are not claims about a device's fixed viewport under every browser/DPI setting. Playwright screenshots for narrow portrait, iPhone SE-class portrait and landscape were opened and visually inspected. Artifacts are under ignored `.remote-testing/browser-results/.../viewport-*.png`.

Physical Galaxy S24 Ultra, iPhone SE 2, iPhone 12 and iPhone 15, real safe-area cutouts, keyboard interruption, font scaling, Safari audio suspension and thermal/frame-rate measurements remain unverified. WebKit is not installed in the local Playwright browser cache. No physical-device performance claim is made.

### P0: Real-player matchmaking

Relevant files: new `apps/server/src/multiplayer/matchmaking.ts`, `RoomService.match`, shared `matchmaking.ts`, web `game/matchmaking.ts`, `MultiplayerMenu.tsx`, and the NGINX route allowlist.

Previously there was no matchmaking implementation. Expected behavior is four distinct authenticated connected participants, genuine waiting status, cancellation and safe reconnect without fabricated participants.

Implemented a server FIFO with at most **128 waiting accounts**, a bounded queue-socket set (capacity plus eight closing/replacement slots), six cancellation messages per ten seconds per actor, the existing 4,096-byte transport cap, 65,536-byte outbound bound and 30-second authorization/heartbeat cleanup. Only current account identities can upgrade; guest mode, forged/missing Origin and URL credentials are rejected. Duplicate account tabs replace the earlier queue connection instead of adding a player. Cancellation/disconnect/logout/expiry remove entries; every send rechecks authorization. Four distinct account identities are validated and assigned synchronously to one existing-architecture room, with no partial assignment if validation/capacity fails. They then use the same ready/start/game protocol as private rooms.

An account reconnecting after a missed assignment gets its already assigned room. Unassigned disconnected accounts rejoin the actual queue; no offline placeholder is counted. If fewer than four are present, the UI shows the actual count and waits. If rooms are full, the queue reports that capacity reason alongside the genuine count and preserves the queue. Browser recovery is bounded; a recovered socket cancels its previous retry timer. No bot/AI seat, fabricated countdown/count, alternate deck or automatic score path was introduced.

Coverage: real HTTP/WebSocket fixtures check four-account atomic assignment, no premature room with one/two/three participants, canonical own-room recovery, cancellation, duplicate tabs, logout/expiry before delivery, guest/Origin/query/strict-payload rejection, capacity release and no partial membership installation. Browser tests use **four distinct local signed OIDC accounts** to cancel, go offline/online, rejoin, receive the same room, ready/start, deal and obtain their own hands. This proves the account/socket architecture with synthetic fixtures, not that four independently observed humans have connected through the public deployment. Actual public Google/TLS/NAT/provider verification remains outstanding.

### P0: Disconnected transition participants

Relevant file: `RoomService.connected` and start/restart/next-round checks. The old check treated presence in `room.peers` as connected until cleanup. A closing transport or inactive session could therefore satisfy a transition's participant check.

Fixed by requiring four member peers whose sockets are OPEN and canonical identities are active. A new regression leaves a closing or revoked peer in the map and verifies **start, restart and next-round** refuse without changing the round or ledger. Disconnection still does not invent forfeits, penalties, replacement humans or bots. Normal current-turn gameplay pauses naturally when that player cannot act; reconnect restores the same authoritative hand/seat.

## Operational limits and priorities

- **P0 rule decisions:** specify the next dealer after an ordinary round; repeated-draw carry; Kapothi+carry; terminal match/tie rules. Confirm accusation/no-trump/replacement policies separately before enabling them.
- **P0 deployment:** use the enforced patched runtime and complete the production readiness report's TLS, real Google registration, historical secret rotation, storage/host/backup and monitoring gates. New NGINX route configuration must be reviewed when actually deploying; nothing was deployed here.
- **P1 acceptance:** conduct a four-human public-network session and physical-phone history, keyboard, safe-area, touch, audio and performance checks. Existing peer-to-peer voice/TURN limitations still apply.
- **P1 lifecycle limits:** rooms and queues remain single-process and memory-only. A restart loses them. Assigned seats are reserved across disconnects; this work does not invent seat eviction or an abandonment/forfeit rule. Existing per-room action/receipt budgets stay bounded; review them against the eventual approved match length. No horizontal scaling or durable-game recovery is claimed.
